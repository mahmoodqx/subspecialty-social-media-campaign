A 34-year-old man with recurrent HLA-B27-positive anterior uveitis presents with right eye pain and blurred vision. His current episode began 5 days ago and he has been using topical prednisolone acetate 1% every 2 hours. Examination reveals 2+ anterior chamber cells, IOP of 38 mmHg, and an open angle on gonioscopy without PAS. What is the most appropriate next step?
A. Add topical aqueous suppressant therapy
B. Perform laser peripheral iridotomy
C. Discontinue topical corticosteroid immediately
D. Initiate oral acetazolamide 500 mg twice daily
Correct Answer A
Category Glaucoma
Topic Inflammatory Glaucoma
Explanation
Answer A is correct because this patient has secondary open-angle glaucoma associated with anterior uveitis, likely from a combination of trabecular meshwork obstruction by inflammatory debris and possible steroid-induced pressure elevation. With an open angle confirmed on gonioscopy and ongoing active inflammation (2+ cells), the appropriate management is to add topical aqueous suppressant therapy while continuing aggressive anti-inflammatory treatment.
Key Concept
Secondary open-angle glaucoma in anterior uveitis results from trabecular meshwork obstruction by inflammatory cells and debris. Gonioscopy is essential to distinguish open-angle from angle-closure mechanisms. Management requires balancing IOP control with continued anti-inflammatory therapy.
